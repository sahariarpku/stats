# Cheat sheet: Study design

| | Observational | Experiment (RCT) |
|---|---|---|
| Treatment | not assigned | **randomly assigned** |
| Can show | association | cause and effect |
| Main threat | confounding | chance, poor blinding |

**Confounder:** linked to both treatment and outcome; fakes or hides effects. **Simpson's paradox:** a trend in every subgroup that reverses when combined (kidney stones: A wins in small 93.1% vs 86.7% and large 73.0% vs 68.8%, but B wins overall 82.6% vs 78.0%).

**Good RCT:** pre-registered question and outcome · random assignment · control or placebo · blinding (single, double) · enough participants · analyse as randomised (intention to treat).

**Bigger sample ≠ less bias.** Self-selected study: estimate −1.4 vs true +5; coin flip: +4.99.

**Sample size (two means, α = 0.05 two-sided, power 80%):**
n per group = 2 (1.96 + 0.84)² σ² ÷ δ² (σ = 10, δ = 5 → 63). Halving δ quadruples n. Decide before collecting data.

**Biases:** selection · volunteer / non-response · survivorship · recall · observer / confirmation · placebo effect.

**Observational ladder:** cross-sectional (snapshot) → case-control (rare diseases) → cohort (follow forward). Association unless many lines of evidence agree.

**Traps:** cause from an observational comparison · "a big n fixes bias" · choosing the outcome after seeing data · ignoring dropouts · "not significant" = "no effect".
