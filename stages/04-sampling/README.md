# Stage 4: Sampling and the Central Limit Theorem

> See why a small sample can tell us about a large population, and how much to trust it.

| Lesson | What it teaches | Time |
|---|---|---|
| [4.1 Sampling Variability and Sampling Distributions](01-sampling-distributions/docs/en.md) | One sample gives one answer. The sampling distribution shows all the answers you could have gotten. | ~35 min |
| [4.2 Standard Error](02-standard-error/docs/en.md) | Standard deviation describes how spread out the data are. Standard error describes how precisely you know the mean. | ~30 min |
| [4.3 The Central Limit Theorem](03-central-limit-theorem/docs/en.md) | Average enough values, from almost any population, and the averages form a bell curve. | ~35 min |
| [4.4 Sample Proportions](04-sample-proportions/docs/en.md) | Polls, defect rates and click-through rates are all proportions. They follow the same sampling logic as means. | ~35 min |
| [4.5 The Bootstrap (Bonus)](05-bootstrap/docs/en.md) | No formula for your statistic? Resample your own data thousands of times and look at how it varies. | ~30 min |

**Stage time:** about 2 h 45 min across 5 lessons.

Every lesson folder contains the lesson (`docs/en.md`), runnable code (`code/`), animations (`visuals/`), a quiz (`quiz.json`) and a one-page cheat sheet (`outputs/cheat-sheet.md`).
